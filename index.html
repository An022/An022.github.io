01

Learning the business side

I began in accounting and audit, where I learned to work through messy information, understand the rules behind a process, and pay attention to details that carry real consequences.

02

Following the automation

I kept noticing repetitive work that software could handle better. Building small automations made me curious about the systems behind them, and eventually led me to study computer science at NYU.

03

Building today

Now I work in software and data, bringing together technical thinking and business context. I especially enjoy tools that reduce friction and make complex workflows easier to trust.

03 / A LITTLE MORE PERSONAL

Curious by nature.
Practical by habit.

I like understanding how things fit together.

That could mean tracing a data problem, shaping a product idea, or learning a new tool just far enough to build something with it. I tend to ask a lot of questions, look for the underlying pattern, and prefer a working experiment over a long hypothetical.

Outside of the final result, I enjoy the middle of the process: the moment when a vague problem starts becoming clear and the pieces begin to connect.
